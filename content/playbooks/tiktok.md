# Playbook — TikTok

Instructions for drafting one TikTok post. TikTok is video-first: default to `reel`/`video` shots unless the brief asks for a photo carousel.
Write in the style guide's language and register; the examples below are English patterns to adapt, not translate.

## One post, one job
- One hook, one engagement mechanic, one call to action.
- Pick the mechanic from the goal:
  - Reach → `share` ("send this to someone who…") or a strong contrarian hook.
  - Comments → `question` (one specific question, answerable in a few words).
  - Returning viewers → `series` (Part 1/2/3). The strongest mechanic on TikTok.
  - Leads → `comment_keyword` only when something real gets sent by DM.
  - Rewatches → `quiz` ("Pause and guess before I say it").
  - Reference content → `save` (steps, numbers, checklists).
- Put the mechanic in `engagement.mechanic` and the exact prompt, keyword or series name in `engagement.detail`.

## The first 2 seconds
Shot 1 is 1–2 s and must stop the scroll on its own, muted.
- `onScreenText` states the hook in ≤ 7 words; `voiceover` says it at the same time.
- Start mid-action: hand on the document, door opening, price on screen. No intro, no logo, no "Hey guys".
Hook formulas (1 line each):
- Contradict a number: "Everyone says 90 days. It took 45."
- Name the mistake: "Don't sign anything before you check this."
- Nobody tells you: "3 things nobody tells you about moving here."
- Before/after: "This is what it cost before. This is now."
- Direct promise: "Your whole checklist in 30 seconds."
- POV (only if natural for the brand): "POV: your first week with a local bank account."
- Reply-style: "You asked if you really need this. Yes. Here's why."

## Structure (`shots`, 15–45 s; up to 60 s only for series or tutorials)
1 Hook (1–2 s) · 2 Context: why it matters, one line · 3–n One point per shot, 2–4 s each · n-1 Payoff: the answer, number or result · n CTA shot (1–2 s)
- Cut every 2–4 s. Change angle, zoom or visual each shot.
- Every shot has `onScreenText` — native-style caption text, ≤ 7 words, placed as if typed in the app. Many watch muted.
- `voiceover`: spoken, conversational, one breath per line (see style guide). Talk to one person.
- Payoff late, not early. A number promised in the hook arrives in the last third.
- Loop ending: the final line flows into the first ("…which is why it's not 90 days." → "Everyone says 90 days.").
- Visual prompts: English, vertical 9:16, handheld/phone-shot feel, real places and objects, no text in frame, no real people's likenesses.

## Native feel, not ads
- Looks like a person made it on a phone: close framing, natural light, quick cuts, no stock-footage gloss.
- Speak to camera or voice over what's on screen. Text overlay is the backbone.
- Trends: use a trending format or sound only when it fits the topic naturally and the brand voice. Never force a meme onto legal, financial or health info. Name the format in the visual prompt; do not name copyrighted songs.

## Photo carousels (`slides`, 7–10) — when the brief asks
TikTok photo mode: slide 1 hook overlay, slides 2–(n-1) one step or point each (≤ 20 words), last slide CTA. Arcs: Steps (hook → why → steps → mistake → recap → CTA) or Myth vs fact (myth → truth with source → what to do → CTA).

## Comments and reply-with-video
- Ask one specific question in `cta` that invites short answers: "Which document took you the longest?"
- Plan follow-ups: good questions become reply videos. In `firstComment`, pin a question or "Part 2 answers the most common question below."
- For reply-video drafts, shot 1 `onScreenText` quotes the viewer's question (paraphrased, no usernames).

## Comment-keyword CTAs
- Only with a real deliverable (checklist, price list, booking link).
- One keyword, one word, ALL CAPS, easy to spell: GUIDE, LIST, PRICE.
- "Comment LIST and I'll DM you the checklist." Keyword alone in `engagement.detail`.

## Series
- Default for anything with more than ~5 points. "Part 1" in hook `onScreenText` and caption first line.
- End every part with the teaser for the next: "Part 2: the step everyone skips."
- Recurring formats work: "One mistake a day", "Price check Friday". Name it in `engagement.detail`.

## Save and share
- Saveable: steps, numbers, deadlines, checklists. CTA: "Save this for when you start."
- Shareable: "Send this to the friend who's thinking about it."
- One of them, in `cta`, never both.

## Caption
- Short: 1–3 lines. First line repeats or extends the hook with a keyword people search for (TikTok is a search engine).
- Add the one piece of context the video can't show. Then the CTA.
- Max ~150 characters before hashtags unless the brief says otherwise.

## Hashtags (`hashtags`, without #)
- 3–5. Specific topic tags + 1–2 local tags (city, country, community in the audience's language).
- No fyp, foryou, viral, trending — they add nothing. No unrelated trending tags.

## Stories (`storyFrames`)
TikTok stories are rarely the right choice; if asked: 3–5 frames, text-led, poll → question → link.

## First comment and alt text
- `firstComment`: a pinned question, the source, or the next-part pointer. Not a hashtag dump.
- `altText`: one or two sentences describing what's shown and the key on-screen text.

## Do not
- Engagement bait: "Like if you agree", "Follow for part 2" without a part 2, "Comment 🔥", "Tag a friend".
- Fake urgency ("Only today!") or invented scarcity.
- Unverifiable claims: guarantees, "100% legal", "the fastest". Laws, fees and deadlines only from FACTS, cited in `sources`.
- Long intros, logo animations, slow reveals of nothing.
- Reposting an Instagram-style polished ad; rewrite for native feel.
- Walls of on-screen text; text inside image or video prompts.
